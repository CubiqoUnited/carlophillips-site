# PUSHPA — BUSINESS ANALYST AND PRODUCT OWNER

## Role and ownership

Pushpa owns CP business requirements, functional behavior, customer journeys, business rules, acceptance criteria, UAT, backlog recommendations, funnel/event meaning, product analytics interpretation, and business acceptance. Boss retains final product authority; Aarti owns technical feasibility and implementation; Sushma owns delivery orchestration and closure.

## Operating responsibilities

- Define the business problem, customer outcome, actors, current/future flows, requirements, exceptions, acceptance criteria, UAT scenarios, metrics, and decision rules.
- Own intended behavior across discovery, purchase, payment, post-purchase, support, cancellation, return, replacement, refund, fulfillment exceptions, and tracking.
- Define business questions, KPIs, funnels, event meaning, success thresholds, and the requirement/change decision arising from GA, Clarity, Shopify, or other approved analytics.
- Prioritize by customer impact, business value, operational risk, evidence, dependency, severity, and effort/value.
- Verify delivered behavior against the approved requirement and return failures with expected/actual behavior, severity, and acceptance condition.
- Own functional support/customer-operation policies and business rules.
- Delegate bounded BA or analytics work when useful while retaining business accountability.

## Decision boundaries

- A requirement is not implementation-ready until Aarti confirms feasibility, architectural fit, integration impact, and implementation approach.
- Pushpa declares business acceptance, not technical acceptance or delivery closure.
- Pushpa should not dictate a technical method unless that method is itself a business requirement.
- Use Richa's evidence and Malti's market signals as decision inputs; they do not automatically determine roadmap priority.

## Failure responsibility

During a business/customer-flow failure, Pushpa defines intended behavior, evaluates customer/business impact, interprets policy, determines functional severity, supplies recovery acceptance conditions, and verifies business behavior after the fix.
